Beyond Anatomy: Diffusion, Perfusion, BOLD, Angiography, Spectroscopy

Everything so far has produced pictures of where the protons are and how they relax. This chapter is about the second half of MRI's power: encoding motion, oxygenation, flow and chemistry into the same phase and amplitude you already know how to control. Each technique below is a small, clever modification of the sequences in Chapter 11.

13.1  Diffusion: making Brownian motion visible

Water molecules in tissue move randomly, a few micrometres in tens of milliseconds. To detect that, apply a strong gradient pulse to wind a position-dependent phase, wait, then apply an equal and opposite pulse to unwind it. A stationary spin ends with exactly zero net phase. A spin that moved between the two pulses does not — and because the movement is random, the population acquires a spread of phases, and the signal drops.

13.1 S = S0 e−b·ADC      b = γ²G²δ²(Δ − δ/3) The b-value (s/mm²) collects the gradient strength G, its duration δ and the separation Δ between the two lobes — it is how hard you are asking the question. The apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC, mm²/s) is the answer the tissue gives. Clinical brain DWI uses b = 0 and b = 1000; ADC is then simply ln(S₀/S₁)/1000, computed per pixel.

From one direction to six, and beyond

Diffusion is directional. In white matter, water moves easily along axons and poorly across myelin sheaths, so the measurement depends on which way the gradient points. Applying diffusion gradients along at least six non-collinear directions lets you fit a 3×3 diffusion tensor, from which follow:

  • Mean diffusivity — the rotationally-invariant average, effectively a directionless ADC.
  • Fractional anisotropy (FA) — 0 for isotropic diffusion, approaching 1 for a perfectly directional structure. A sensitive but non-specific marker of white matter integrity.
  • Principal eigenvector — the dominant direction of diffusion, conventionally colour-coded red (left–right), green (anterior–posterior), blue (superior–inferior). Following it from voxel to voxel is tractography.
What tractography is and is not

Tractography follows a diffusion direction field. It does not see axons, cannot determine direction of signal flow, and fails wherever fibres cross — which is a large fraction of white matter. The tensor model allows only one direction per voxel; higher-order models (HARDI, Q-ball, constrained spherical deconvolution, NODDI) exist precisely because that assumption is inadequate. Beautiful rainbow tract renderings are model output, not measurement, and should be read with that in mind.

13.3  BOLD and functional MRI

Neural activity increases local blood flow more than it increases oxygen consumption. The result is a net decrease in deoxyhaemoglobin, which is paramagnetic; less of it means less local field distortion, which means a longer T2*, which means slightly more signal on a T2*-weighted gradient-echo EPI. The effect is roughly 0.5–3% at 3 T — smaller than the noise in any single image, and recovered only by statistics over hundreds of volumes.

13.5  Spectroscopy: chemistry instead of anatomy

Switch the gradients off during readout and the only thing separating frequencies is chemical shift. The result is a spectrum rather than an image: peaks whose positions identify metabolites and whose areas measure concentration. Because metabolites are 10 000× less abundant than water, MRS needs large voxels (typically 1–8 cm³), long acquisitions, excellent shimming, and aggressive water suppression.

Key points

  • Diffusion: S = S₀e−b·ADC. Always read DWI with its ADC map to avoid T2 shine-through.
  • The tensor gives FA and fibre direction, with a hard limit: one direction per voxel.
  • Perfusion by bolus (DSC/DCE) or by labelling blood itself (ASL); the DWI–perfusion mismatch guides stroke treatment.
  • BOLD is a 1–3% T2* effect, blurred by a several-second haemodynamic response.
  • Three MRA mechanisms: inflow, velocity-phase, and T1 shortening. Phase contrast is the quantitative one.
  • Spectroscopy removes gradients during readout so chemical shift alone separates signals.
  • Quantitative MRI replaces arbitrary intensities with physical units — the direction the field is moving.
