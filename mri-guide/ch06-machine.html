Part II · The Instrument

The Machine: Magnet, Gradients, RF Chain, Shields

Everything so far has been physics on paper. This chapter is what is actually in the room: seven tonnes of superconducting magnet, a kilolitre of liquid helium, three gradient coils driven by amplifiers the size of a wardrobe, and a copper cage keeping the local FM station out of your images. If you have never stood next to one, this is the chapter to read slowly.

6.1  What a scanner actually is, layer by layer

A cylindrical-bore scanner is a set of nested tubes. From the outside in, each layer solves one problem, and each layer takes space away from the patient — which is why the bore ends up being only 60–70 cm across in a machine that is nearly 2 m in diameter.

6.2  The magnet

Clinical field strengths of 1.5 T and above are only achievable with superconductivity. The coil is niobium–titanium wire embedded in copper, wound into solenoids, and cooled to about 4.2 K by liquid helium. Below its critical temperature the wire has literally zero resistance, so once the current is established — typically 400–800 A — the power supply is disconnected and a persistent-mode switch closes the loop on itself. The current then circulates for years, decaying by less than 0.1 ppm per hour. A scanner's magnet is not switched off at night; it is on continuously from installation until decommissioning.

Quench

If any part of the winding warms above its critical temperature, it becomes resistive. That resistance dissipates heat, which spreads the normal zone, which dissipates more heat — a runaway that dumps the entire stored magnetic energy (megajoules) into the helium bath in seconds. The helium boils and expands roughly 750-fold, and must leave through the quench pipe to the outside air; if that pipe is blocked, the magnet room fills with helium and becomes an asphyxiation hazard. A quench is loud, expensive (tens of thousands in helium and days of downtime), and is also the emergency stop: the Emergency Run Down Unit deliberately triggers one when a person is pinned by a ferromagnetic object. Modern “sealed” magnets use only 5–20 litres of helium with a cryocooler, which changes this risk profile substantially.

Homogeneity and shimming

The Larmor equation says frequency is field. If the field varies across the imaging volume, then position and frequency are no longer the clean relationship that Chapter 7 depends on. Specifications are quoted as peak-to-peak variation over a diameter of spherical volume (DSV): typically < 1 ppm over 40 cm DSV, which at 3 T means holding 3 teslas constant to within 3 microtesla across a volume the size of a basketball.

  • Passive shimming — small steel plates slid into trays in the bore during installation, correcting the effect of the building's steel and the magnet's own manufacturing tolerances. Done once, iteratively, over hours.
  • Active shimming — dedicated resistive coils generating specific spherical-harmonic field terms (Z, Z², ZX, X²−Y², …), adjusted per patient and even per slice. Modern systems shim automatically before each acquisition, because the patient's own body — especially air in sinuses and bowel — distorts the field significantly.
  • Higher-order / dynamic shimming — updating shim currents per slice during the scan. Essential for EPI at 7 T, where the field over the frontal lobes is hopeless otherwise.

Fringe field: the part that reaches outside the room

The field does not stop at the cover. Every scanner is specified by its 5 gauss line (0.5 mT) — the contour outside which pacemakers and other implants are considered safe, and which must be contained within the controlled area. Unshielded, that contour would reach ten metres or more; active shielding (a second, counter-wound superconducting coil outside the main one) pulls it in to typically 2.5–4 m axially, which is what makes siting a scanner in an ordinary hospital possible at all.

6.3  The gradient system

Three coils produce fields whose z-component varies linearly with position along x, y and z respectively. Note the wording carefully: a gradient coil does not produce a field pointing along x; it produces a field pointing along z whose strength depends on x. Only the z-component matters, because it adds to a 3 T field and everything perpendicular is negligible by comparison.

Why the scanner is so loud

A gradient coil carries hundreds of amperes inside a 3 T field. The Lorentz force on a current-carrying wire in a field is F = I L × B, which here amounts to tonnes of force trying to push the coil apart, reversing polarity thousands of times per second. The coil former flexes; the flexing radiates sound. That is all the knocking is. Quiet sequences work by making the gradient waveforms smooth (sinusoidal rather than trapezoidal), reducing dB/dt and therefore the impulsive force — at the cost of longer readouts.

Eddy currents and pre-emphasis

A switching gradient induces currents in every conducting surface nearby — the cryostat bore tube above all. Those eddy currents produce their own decaying fields that distort the intended waveform, causing image distortion, ghosting and shifted slices. Two fixes are used together: active shielding (extra windings that null the field outside the gradient coil) and pre-emphasis (deliberately over-driving the amplifier with an exponentially decaying correction so that the net field is the rectangle you wanted). Pre-emphasis constants are measured at installation and are part of routine service calibration.

Key points

  • A scanner is nested cylinders: cryostat, shim, gradients, RF body coil, bore. Radius is a zero-sum resource.
  • The magnet is always on, running in persistent mode; a quench is both a failure mode and the emergency stop.
  • Homogeneity of ~1 ppm over 40 cm is achieved by passive plus active shimming, re-optimised per patient.
  • Gradients make Bz vary with position — amplitude sets resolution, slew rate sets speed, and human nerves set the ceiling.
  • Acoustic noise is Lorentz force on the gradient windings, nothing more exotic.
  • Transmit big and uniform; receive small and close. That asymmetry is the design of the whole RF chain.
  • The room is a Faraday cage because the signal is microvolts at broadcast-radio frequencies.
