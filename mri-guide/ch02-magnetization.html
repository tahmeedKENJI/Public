Part I · The Physics

Magnetization, Zeeman Splitting and Larmor Precession

Put a body in a strong magnet and two things happen. A vanishingly small majority of spins settles into the lower energy state, giving the tissue a net magnetic vector; and every one of those spins begins to precess at a frequency set only by the field. Everything after this chapter is manipulation of that vector at that frequency.

2.1  The Zeeman interaction

A magnetic moment in a field has energy E = −μ·B. For a spin-½ nucleus the z-component of μ takes only two values, ±½γℏ, so the field splits one energy level into two:

2.1 ΔE = γℏB₀ = h · (γ/2π) · B₀ = h f0 The energy gap is exactly a photon at the Larmor frequency. That is the whole resonance condition: irradiate at f₀ and you can drive transitions; irradiate at anything else and you cannot. Everything about MRI's frequency selectivity descends from this one line.

2.2  Boltzmann: how few spins actually count

At thermal equilibrium the two states are populated according to the Boltzmann distribution. Because ΔE ≪ kT by five orders of magnitude, the exponential linearises and the fractional excess in the lower state is simply:

2.2 (N↑ − N↓) / N ≈ γℏB₀ / (2kT) Linear in B₀ — double the field, double the signal source. Inversely proportional to temperature, which is why hyperpolarisation techniques that create a non-thermal population difference can gain four to five orders of magnitude.

Summing the moments of that excess over a unit volume containing N spins gives the equilibrium magnetization, the quantity that the rest of MRI treats as its raw material:

2.3 M0 = N γ²ℏ²B₀ / (4kT)   (spin ½) Note the γ² here, and remember from Chapter 9 that the induced voltage in the receive coil brings a further factor of ω₀ ∝ γB₀. Signal ∝ B₀² per unit time is the usual quoted scaling before noise is taken into account.

2.3  Precession: why the vector turns instead of falling over

A compass needle in a field swings until it points along the field and stops. A nuclear moment does not, because it carries angular momentum. The field exerts a torque τ = μ × B, and torque equals rate of change of angular momentum, so with μ = γJ:

2.4 dμ/dt = γ (μ × B) The change is always perpendicular to both μ and B. A vector whose derivative is perpendicular to itself cannot change length; it can only rotate. The solution is precession about B at a fixed angle, exactly like a gyroscope under gravity.
2.5 ω0 = γ B₀   ⟺   f0 = (γ/2π) B₀   — the Larmor equation Independent of the angle between μ and B, independent of temperature, independent of everything except γ and the field the nucleus locally experiences. That last qualifier is the hook on which all of spatial encoding hangs (Chapter 7).
The single most useful reframing

Stop thinking about spins. From here on, think about M, one classical vector per voxel, obeying dM/dt = γ M × B. Every pulse sequence in Chapter 11 is a recipe for pushing that vector around. The quantum description is still true underneath, but it buys you nothing until you get to relaxation mechanisms and spectroscopy — and it costs you the ability to visualise anything.

2.4  Longitudinal and transverse: the only decomposition you need

Split M into the component along B₀ and the component perpendicular to it:

  • Mz, longitudinal magnetization. Parallel to B₀. It represents stored, recoverable order. It is invisible — a constant vector along the bore axis induces no voltage in any coil. Its recovery is governed by T1.
  • Mxy, transverse magnetization. Perpendicular to B₀ and precessing at ω₀. This is the only thing a receive coil can detect, because it is the only part that changes with time. Its decay is governed by T2 and T2*.

The entire craft of pulse sequence design is a scheduling problem over these two quantities: convert Mz into Mxy at the right moment, read Mxy before it decays, and let Mz recover enough before you do it again.

Field strength is not free

Going from 1.5 T to 3 T doubles M₀, but it also doubles the chemical shift in Hz (worse fat–water misregistration), quadruples RF power deposition for the same flip angle (SAR ∝ B₀², Chapter 15), shortens T2* (more susceptibility dephasing), lengthens T1 (which costs you signal back on T1-weighted work), and makes the RF wavelength in tissue comparable to the head, causing standing-wave B1 shading. High field is a trade, not an upgrade.

Key points

  • ΔE = γℏB₀ = hf₀. The resonance condition and the Larmor frequency are the same statement.
  • Polarisation ≈ γℏB₀/2kT — about 5 ppm at 1.5 T. Everything is built to detect that sliver.
  • ω₀ = γB₀, independent of angle. The torque equation makes M rotate, never settle.
  • M₀ ∝ B₀, and the detected signal picks up another factor of ω₀ from Faraday induction.
  • Only Mxy is detectable. Mz is stored potential, converted by RF.
  • Equilibrium has no signal because the spins have random phase. Coherence, not alignment, is what a pulse creates.
